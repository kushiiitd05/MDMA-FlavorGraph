Serving assumed: **2 boiled eggs (100 g)** vs **150 g cooked rice + 150 g cooked toor daal**.

| Per serving | 2 boiled eggs (100 g) | Rice 150 g + daal 150 g |
|---|---|---|

| Protein | 12.6 g | 14.2 g |
| Fat | 10.6 g | 1.0 g |
| Carbohydrate | 1.1 g | 77.1 g |
| Fibre | **0 g** | **10.7 g** |
| Iron | 1.2 mg | 3.5 mg |
| Folate | 44 µg | 254 µg |
| Vitamin B12 | **1.11 µg** | **0 µg** |
| Vitamin D | **2.2 µg** | **0 µg** |
| Choline | **294 mg** | ~2 mg (rice) |
| Selenium | 30.8 µg | 15.6 µg |

**Argument (write ~6 lines):**
1. The egg is **nutrient-dense but energy-light**: complete protein in a small mass, plus B12, vitamin D, choline and selenium — micronutrients rice + daal supply essentially **zero** of. Its protein is the reference-quality protein (DIAAS/PDCAAS ≈ 1.0).
2. Rice + daal delivers **more total protein, ~2.4× the energy, 10× the fibre, ~3× the iron and ~6× the folate**, with almost no fat.
3. Neither is a complete protein alone, but together they are: **cereal protein is lysine-poor / methionine-adequate, pulse protein is lysine-rich / methionine-poor**, so the combination is *mutually complementary* — the nutritional logic behind dal-chawal. *(IFCT 2017; FAO/WHO amino-acid scoring)*
4. Caveats: daal iron is **non-haem** and poorly absorbed, and both rice and daal carry **phytate**, which inhibits iron/zinc uptake; egg iron is also non-haem and egg contains no vitamin C to aid absorption.

**Verdict:** *Neither is universally "healthier."* They are **complementary**, not competing: the egg fills the B12/D/choline gap of a plant meal; rice + daal supplies the fibre, folate and bulk energy an egg cannot.

## Q6 · Scientific truth or myth?  [3 marks]

**Answer each as: claim → mechanism → evidence → conditions → verdict.**

### (a) "Cooking food in a microwave destroys its nutritional value" — **MYTH (as stated), but nutrient-specific**

- **Mechanism.** Microwaves heat by **dielectric heating** of water dipoles. This is *thermal*; the photon energy (~1 µeV at 2.45 GHz) is far too low to break chemical bonds or ionise. Nutrient loss in *any* cooking is driven by three things only: **temperature, time, and water contact (leaching)** — not by the heating modality.
- **Evidence.** Yuan et al. (2009), broccoli: vitamin C loss — **boiling 33 %, stir-fry 24 %, microwaving 16 %, steaming ≈ 0 %**. Because a microwave uses little/no water and short times, it usually **preserves water-soluble vitamins better than boiling**.
- **But the opposite for other compounds:** in the same study **microwaving caused the largest loss of aliphatic glucosinolates (60 %)**, while steaming left them almost unchanged.
- **Conditions.** Result depends on the nutrient, added water, power and duration; over-microwaving to dryness destroys nutrients just as over-boiling does.
- **Verdict.** **Myth as a blanket claim.** Microwaving is generally *equal to or better than boiling* for vitamin C, *worse than steaming* for glucosinolates. No modality "destroys nutrition" per se.

### (b) "Refrigerating food destroys its nutritional value" — **MYTH in framing; a real but small, slow effect**

- **Mechanism.** Cold **slows** — does not stop — enzymatic and oxidative degradation (Q₁₀ effect). Ascorbate is oxidised by ascorbate oxidase in the presence of O₂; light and bruising accelerate it. Refrigeration simultaneously **suppresses microbial spoilage**, which otherwise destroys the food entirely.
- **Evidence.** Salad leaves lost **35–86 % of ascorbate over 10 days at 4 °C**; refrigerated spinach lost ≈29 % in 1 day. Losses are **time-dependent**, and are **far larger at room temperature** than in the fridge.
- **Conditions.** Affects mainly **labile** nutrients — vitamin C, folate, some carotenoid isomers. **Protein, fat, carbohydrate, minerals, and vitamins A/D/E/K are essentially unaffected.**
- **Verdict.** **Myth as stated.** Refrigeration *retards* nutrient loss relative to ambient storage; the loss that does occur is caused by **storage time**, not by cold. (Blanch-freezing preserves labile vitamins better still.)

### (c) "Genetic modifications in plants or animals are 'bad'" — **MYTH as a blanket claim; must be assessed case-by-case**

- **The claim is malformed:** "GM" is a **technique**, not an exposure. Risk attaches to the **trait and the specific product**, not to the method. Conventional breeding, mutagenesis and gene editing all alter genomes.
- **Evidence.** The **US National Academies (2016), *Genetically Engineered Crops: Experiences and Prospects*** reviewed ~900 studies and found **no substantiated evidence of a difference in human-health risk** between commercialised GE crops (soy, maize, cotton) and conventionally bred counterparts — no link to cancer, infertility, GI or metabolic disease. **WHO/FAO/Codex** concur that no adverse health effects have been reported from GM foods on the market, and each product is assessed individually under Codex guidelines.
- **Legitimate, separate concerns** (agronomic/socio-economic, *not* food-safety): **herbicide-resistant weed evolution** and insect resistance from over-reliance on a single trait; **gene flow** to wild relatives; seed-patent/market-concentration issues; and the need for allergenicity testing when a novel protein is introduced (e.g. the Brazil-nut 2S albumin soybean, which was **caught by testing and never marketed** — evidence the safety system works).
- **Verdict.** **Myth** that GM is inherently bad. Scientific consensus: currently marketed GM foods are as safe to eat as their conventional counterparts; **each new trait still requires its own risk assessment**, and the real debates are ecological and economic rather than toxicological.

## Q7 · Measuring the calorific content of food  [2 marks] — *write 5–8 lines*

> **Bomb calorimetry.**
> A **known, dry mass** of food is sealed inside a strong steel "bomb" **pressurised with excess oxygen (~25–30 atm)** and immersed in a **measured mass of water** inside an insulated calorimeter.
> The sample is ignited electrically and undergoes **complete combustion**; the heat released warms the bomb and the surrounding water.
> A stirrer keeps the water uniform and a precision thermometer records the **temperature rise ΔT**.
> The calorimeter's **heat capacity C** is found beforehand by burning a standard of known energy (**benzoic acid**).
> The **gross energy** is then **Q = C × ΔT**, divided by sample mass to give kcal (or kJ) per gram.
> **Important caveat:** this is *gross* combustion energy. Humans do not fully digest or absorb everything, and protein is excreted as urea rather than fully oxidised, so **food labels use metabolisable energy** — obtained by applying **Atwater factors (4, 9, 4 kcal/g for protein, fat, carbohydrate; 2 kcal/g for fibre)**, which are lower than bomb values.

## Q8 · Eight technologies/products from Computational Gastronomy  [8 marks]

*(1–3 lines each; each must be narrow and technically distinct — avoid "AI cooking app".)*

1. **Process-conditioned flavour-substitution engine.** Recommends an ingredient swap *conditioned on the cooking process it will undergo* — because a raw substitution and a caramelised one are different flavour problems. Ranks candidates by shared volatile-compound profile **after** the specified thermal treatment.

2. **Food-state-aware mistake-recovery assistant.** Takes the *current* state of a dish ("curry over-salted", "custard split", "dough over-proved") and returns evidence-backed corrective actions, ranked by how reversible the underlying physico-chemical change is (emulsion vs. denaturation vs. Maillard).

3. **Long-tail regional-ingredient identifier.** Recognises vernacular and hyperlocal ingredient names (kachri, sohphlang, ker-sangri) across scripts and dialect spellings and maps them to a canonical ontology node with composition data — the coverage gap in every existing food database.

4. **Aroma-driven end-point (doneness) predictor.** Uses a low-cost MOS/electronic-nose sensor array over the pan to predict the **time-to-optimal-endpoint** from the evolving volatile signature (e.g. pyrazine onset in a roast), rather than from a fixed timer.

5. **Cuisine-authenticity pattern analyser.** Scores how well a recipe's ingredient-pair and spice-combination statistics match a target regional cuisine's empirical signature, and names the *specific* pairs responsible for the deviation.

6. **Nutrition-constrained recipe transformation system.** Rewrites an existing recipe to satisfy hard nutritional constraints (≤ X mg sodium, ≥ Y g protein, low-FODMAP) as a **constrained optimisation over the ingredient graph**, minimising flavour-profile drift from the original.

7. **Evidence-grounded cooking-process substitution engine.** Given equipment or dietary limits, substitutes the *process* rather than the ingredient (deep-fry → air-fry → oven-roast), predicting the resulting change in texture, moisture loss and nutrient retention from published process-kinetics data.

8. **Leftover-state food-waste recovery planner.** Reasons over what is in the fridge **plus how far each item has degraded** (day-3 cooked rice ≠ fresh rice: *B. cereus* risk, retrograded starch) and proposes dishes whose processes are appropriate to that state, ordered by spoilage urgency.
